Five free tools to shape how your organization uses AI

  • Free and public. They are yours to keep.
  • Co-produced. Intentional Data co-produced them with community organizations.
  • How it works. Each tool asks you questions one at a time, and you answer in your own words.
  • Works in any AI tool. Install the tools in Claude or ChatGPT, or copy the prompts into any AI tool.
  • Custom files that guide your AI. Your values, voice and folder files shape how AI works for you from here on, and your policy and commitment give your team and board something to adopt.

Click a tool to see an example of what you get, then choose how to use it.

Tool 1Start here

Ethics tool

  • values.md

You put your organization's values into words, and get a values check you can use on any draft, tool or plan.

How it works: You answer questions about your work, one at a time, and the tool helps you put your organization's values into words. It writes them down for you to review. After that, it works as your values check. You can ask it whether a draft, a tool or a plan lines up with what you wrote.

Tool 2

Voice tool

  • voice-profile.md

Your writing sounds like you, even when AI helps with the draft.

How it works: You answer questions about your values, by typing or by talking, and the tool helps you decide how you want to sound. After that, you can give it a piece of writing whose content you are happy with, and it adjusts the style so it sounds like you. Your facts, numbers and sources stay exactly as they are.

Tool 3

Folder tool

  • about-us.md
  • use-cases.md
  • tool-list.md
  • data-rules.md
  • read-me-first.md

Your team builds one shared folder that any AI tool can read, so every new chat starts with your organization's context.

How it works: You ask your AI a real question before you have a folder. Then the tool builds the folder with you, one file at a time, and you approve every line. At the end you ask the same question again and compare the two answers. A second version adds extra steps for teams that build AI tools for others.

Tool 4

AI Use Policy tool

  • sorting.md
  • use-policy.md

Your team agrees on which AI uses are fine, which need a pause and which are off limits, and gets a policy draft in its own words.

How it works: It walks your team through the ways you use AI, or want to, one question at a time. For each one, your team decides who benefits, who could be harmed, and whether it is fine, needs a pause or is off limits. Then it drafts your AI use policy in your words.

Tool 5

Leadership Commitment tool

  • leadership-commitment.md

Your leaders name who is accountable for AI, who can pause a tool and how people are told.

How it works: It reads your use policy, then asks your leaders who is responsible for what: who is accountable for AI at your organization, how often they report, who can pause a tool, and how people are told when AI touches their case.

What you will need

  • About 20 to 30 minutes per tool

# Maple Street Neighbors: Our values

Draft | Date: November 9, 2026 | Review date: May 9, 2027

## Who this statement is for

Our funders, our volunteers and the neighbors we serve.

## Neighbors lead

**What it means to us:** The people who use our pantry tell us what the pantry should offer.

**On an ordinary Tuesday:** Before we place the weekly order, we ask the people in line what is missing from the shelves.

**What we will stop doing:** Choosing programs from a funder's menu before we ask the block.

## Every neighbor gets the same welcome

**What it means to us:** Everyone who walks in is greeted by name and asked how we can help, in the language they prefer.

**On an ordinary Tuesday:** A volunteer greets each person at the door and offers the same choices to everyone.

**What we will stop doing:** Asking people to prove need before they can take food.

Each quarter, we read this page at our team meeting. We name one thing we did that matched our values and one thing we missed.

**Stories:** The winter we moved the pantry hours after neighbors told us the old hours clashed with night shifts.

**Hard case:** Keeping Saturday hours cost two volunteers' time and a part-time stipend. The team kept the hours.

**Disagreements:** One volunteer wanted a sign-in sheet. The team has not settled it.

**Community input plan:** Ask three neighbors who use the pantry to read the draft and pay each of them for their time.

use-policy.md From the AI Use Policy tool
# Maple Street Neighbors AI use policy

**Draft: not yet adopted.** Date: November 19, 2026. In the conversation: director, two volunteers, communications lead. Review date: May 19, 2027.

## Why we have this policy

Neighbors lead. We want every use of AI to start from our own words and stay under our own control.

## What we use AI for

We draft our monthly newsletter from our own notes. We summarize grant reports we wrote ourselves. A person on our team reads every draft before it goes out.

## What we pause and ask about

Turning volunteer sign-up sheets into a schedule. The director decides after two volunteers have reviewed what goes into the tool.

## What we do not use AI for

Summarizing case notes. Writing anything that quotes a neighbor without their say.

## Data in and never in

Never in: names, addresses or phone numbers of the people we serve, case notes, and funder reports with financial details. May go in once names and details are removed: event descriptions and our own newsletter drafts.

## People in the loop

The communications lead checks every AI draft before it goes out. Two neighbors who use the pantry read our newsletter each quarter and tell us what felt off.

## Community harm is a red flag

Any team member can call a pause when a neighbor reports harm or misrepresentation. The report goes to the director.

## Vendors and models

Before we adopt a tool we review it on the 20-criteria rubric and save the result as tool-evaluation.md.

## Telling people

We tell a neighbor when AI touched something that was about them, in plain words, at the time.

## Raising a concern

A staff member, volunteer or partner tells the director, or the board chair if the concern is about the director. No one is penalized for raising a concern.

## Review

Every six months. The director leads.

What it does
Your AI tool may remember some things from past chats, and the tool decides what it keeps. The Folder tool helps your team build one shared folder that you write, read and edit, which holds what your AI needs to know, and one file that tells any AI which files to read first. Deciding what your AI reads before it answers is called context engineering.
What you bring
Your values.md from the Ethics tool. Your voice-profile.md from the Voice tool is welcome too, and you can add it later. Bring your most recent statement of work, your grant application narrative or short descriptions of your programs, products or services, and your branding. Set aside about 30 minutes.
What you get
Five files in your shared folder: about-us.md, use-cases.md, tool-list.md, data-rules.md and read-me-first.md. The advanced version also makes a folder for each program, product or service. You also get two answers to the same question, one from before your folder and one from after, so you can see what the folder changed.
How you use it
It asks one question at a time and you decide every line. It starts by asking a real question of your AI with no folder. It then builds the folder one file at a time, or one program at a time in the advanced version. At the end it asks the same question again, in the same chat, and you compare the two answers. If a step goes a different way than planned, such as a missing values file, it tells you what to do and where on this page to go.
Two versions
Choose the Folder tool to go deep on who you are: your business, purpose, rules, voice, past and stories. Choose the advanced version if you already know who you are and want to spend your time on how your system runs. It gives each program, product or service its own folder, a second brain with a part that thinks, a part that acts and a part that looks things up.
What it feeds
It starts from your values.md and voice-profile.md. The AI Use Policy tool reads use-cases.md, data-rules.md and tool-list.md from your folder.
