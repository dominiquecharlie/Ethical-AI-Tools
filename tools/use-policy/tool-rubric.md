# Tool evaluation rubric: 20 criteria

Five categories, four criteria each. Score every criterion from 0 to 3.

## Scale

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Does not meet | The tool shows major gaps against the criterion. A criterion the team skips, or the vendor's pages do not cover, is marked skipped and is not scored. |
| 1 | Partly meets, with gaps | Limited capability or partial commitments. The team needs a written plan to close the gaps before using it. |
| 2 | Meets the standard | The tool meets the requirement with documentation, checked safeguards and clear alignment with the team's values. |
| 3 | Exceeds the standard | Continuous, independent or community-led verification, or third-party certification. |

## Categories and default weights

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|
| Fiscal responsibility and sustainability (FR) | 15% |
| Ethics, privacy and data security (EP) | 25% |
| Human-centeredness and autonomy (HC) | 20% |
| Environmental and social responsibility (ES) | 15% |
| Community impact and interrogative reasoning (CI) | 25% |

The team can change the weights. They must add up to 100%.

## Criteria

| ID | Criterion | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| FR-1 | Total cost of ownership | Licenses, training, setup and upkeep, set against the time the tool saves. Who owns the output. |
| FR-2 | Resource footprint and financial sustainability | Long-term budget stability and exposure to price or rule changes. |
| FR-3 | Vendor termination and lock-in | Data portability, contract termination and how easy it is to leave. Whether you can delete your data and export your work, how long deleted data is kept, and any legal holds. |
| FR-4 | Operational core alignment | Fit with the organization's real goals and measurable value to the mission. |
| EP-1 | Sensitive data safeguards | Blocking or filtering personal data before it enters the model. Which vendor staff can read your content. |
| EP-2 | Privacy protection and anonymization | Data governance, security and anonymization. Whether your data trains the model on your plan, and how to opt out. |
| EP-3 | Whistleblower protection channels | Confidential reporting channels and protection from retaliation for vendor staff. |
| EP-4 | Standards and certification | Alignment with recognized frameworks such as NIST AI RMF and ISO 42001, and independent certifications. How the vendor handles law enforcement and government data requests. |
| HC-1 | Individual autonomy and human control | People keep control. No deception or unjustified surveillance. |
| HC-2 | Transparency and explainability | Clear notice that AI is in use, how decisions are made and which inputs count. |
| HC-3 | Contestability and human redress | A way to challenge an AI outcome, with a human making the final call. |
| HC-4 | Inclusion, fairness and anti-discrimination | Inclusive, accessible design that avoids unfair discrimination. |
| ES-1 | Environmental resource disclosure | Published energy and resource footprint. |
| ES-2 | Cognitive and skill augmentation | The tool builds people's skills and leaves jobs in people's hands. |
| ES-3 | Diversity in development teams | A vendor commitment to diverse teams across cultures and disciplines. |
| ES-4 | Catastrophic risk and safety assessment | Capability and safety evaluations, public system cards and third-party review. |
| CI-1 | Upfront harm mitigation | Ways to audit and correct outputs for deficit framing, stereotyping and cultural erasure. |
| CI-2 | Collective relational oversight | Support for community-led oversight that reaches beyond one reviewer. |
| CI-3 | Ongoing upstream interrogation | Community questioning of outputs during use, as well as after. |
| CI-4 | Proximity as expertise | Lived experience counts as evidence and has the power to change how the tool is used. |
